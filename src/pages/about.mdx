**Dr. Eric Janusson** — analytical chemist, mass spectrometrist, and scientific software developer.

I have spent more than a decade in mass spectrometry, across academic research, regulated industry
laboratories, and open source. My work has followed a single question — what does it take for a
chemical measurement to be trustworthy? — from the physics of the electrospray droplet, to watching
reactions happen in real time, to the automated pipelines and software that modern analytical
science depends on.

## Research

My doctoral work at the University of Victoria (2012–2017) began at the source itself: the
electrospray droplet. I studied how solvent composition and spray geometry affect electrospray
response and relative ion signals. From there my research
turned to chemistry in motion: using pressurized sample infusion ESI-MS, I followed the activation
of a palladium precatalyst by phosphine ligands in real time. My dissertation developed the mass
spectrometry techniques for monitoring reactions as they happen — a foundation that later supported
step-by-step observation of a catalytic amination.

At the University of Glasgow (2017–2020), as a postdoctoral research associate, I moved from
individual measurements to high-dimensional ones. Coupling travelling-wave ion mobility with
high-resolution mass spectrometry, I studied polyoxometalate self-assembly and isomer selection
using carbohydrate reducing agents. The data quickly outgrew what instrument software could interpret, so I wrote automated
Python pipelines to process it reproducibly. That work convinced me that modern analytical
chemistry is as much a computational discipline as a physical one.

## Industry

At Delic Labs (2021–2022), as Lead Chemist, I worked on laboratory commissioning, method
validation, quality documentation, and an untargeted DIA-LC-MS/MS profiling pipeline.

At TMC Manufacturing (2024–2026), as Principal Scientist for Analytical Development & Laboratory
Operations, I worked on trace-element methods and an ICP-MS capital proposal for high-purity
stable-isotope production. These experiences reinforced the importance of treating instruments,
methods, documentation, and data handling as parts of the same measurement system.

## Software

Today my focus is the software layer of analytical science. [MassFlow](https://github.com/janusson/MassFlow)
is a Python toolkit for local MS/MS library search and candidate annotation; its results are not
confirmed identifications. [MSMCP](https://github.com/janusson/MSMCP) exposes tools for supported
mass spectrometry data and computations to compatible Model Context Protocol clients. Both are
open source; the [projects page](/projects/) describes their scope and limitations.
